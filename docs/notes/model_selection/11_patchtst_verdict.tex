\section{PatchTST: patching vs raw attention}

The hypothesis behind PatchTST (Nie et al., 2023): treating hour-long
price subsequences as tokens (``patches'') reduces the attention cost from
$O(T^2)$ to $O((T/P)^2)$ where $P$ is the patch length. This allows
cheap attention over months of history, which TFT cannot do at the same
compute budget.

\textbf{Key question:} does the patch inductive bias help on this task,
or does it throw away the intraday structure that matters for prices?

\subsection{Design of the sweep}

27 screening configs: $\text{patch\_len} \in \{12, 24, 48\}$ hours,
$\text{stride} \in \{6, 12, 24\}$ hours, $\text{ctx} \in \{672, 1344, 2016\}$
hours (28, 56, 84 days). Fixed: d\_model=64, seed=42, max\_epochs=50 patience=6.

Guard: configs with $n\_\text{patches} > 256$ are skipped (attention cost).
After screening: top-3 configs by val pinball $\to$ walk-forward.

\subsection{Interpretation of patch length}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{patch\_len=12}: patches cover 12 hours (half a day). Captures
        morning vs afternoon vs evening regimes. Preserves intraday shape.
  \item \texttt{patch\_len=24}: patches cover 1 day. Collapses the intraday
        curve into one token. Cheap; loses hour-of-day signal.
  \item \texttt{patch\_len=48}: patches cover 2 days. Captures weekday
        vs weekend patterns as tokens. Very compressed.
\end{itemize}

The merit-order mechanism (solar suppresses hour 11--15; gas peaks hour 19--21)
suggests short patches (12h) should preserve more signal. Long patches trade
signal for context length.

\subsection{Screening results}

27 configs screened (3 skipped: $n_\text{patches} > 256$). 24 completed.
Zero-variance bug fixed before this run: \texttt{wind\_off\_fcst\_mw} was
all-zero in training $\to$ val input became $1.9 \times 10^7$
$\to$ val pinball 879. Fix: zero-out constant columns after z-scoring.
See section~\ref{sec:zero-variance-guard} in the architecture note.

\textbf{Key observations.}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Context = 1344 h (56 days) dominates.} All top-7 configs
        use ctx=1344. Ctx=672 consistently lands in the bottom half.
        Conclusion: 8 weeks of price history is better than 4 weeks.
        Ctx=2016 (84 days) loses to 56 days -- possibly because the sample
        count drops too low for the longer receptive field to pay off.

  \item \textbf{Patch length 24 h wins, not 12 h.} I expected 12 h patches
        to win because intraday price shape (solar noon dip, evening peak)
        matters. Instead, 24 h patches topped the ranking. Likely reason:
        one patch = one calendar day makes weekday/weekend and holiday
        patterns the natural token-level signal. The model can then attend
        across 56 day-tokens and pick up the week rhythm.

  \item \textbf{Stride does not matter much at ctx=1344.} Rank 1 uses
        stride=24 h (non-overlapping), rank 2 uses stride=12 h. The 0.002
        difference is within noise for a single-seed screen.

  \item \textbf{Best PatchTST val (0.1236) vs best TFT HPO val (0.1157).}
        PatchTST trails by 0.007 on the screening split. This does not
        settle the walk-forward comparison -- TFT HPO val 0.1157 became
        walk-forward rMAE 0.706 (worse than LEAR 0.653). PatchTST has
        $\sim$197k params vs TFT 1.27M, so may generalise better.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Verdict: negative.} PatchTST did not clear the TFT gate
(22.98 vs 19.71 EUR/MWh). Coverage collapsed to 69.5\% against a
target of 80\%. Spike MAE 79.3 vs LGBM 60.7.

Why did screening val predict the wrong ranking? The screening split
(2026-01-01 cut) rewards models that memorise 2025 regime. Walk-forward
uses monthly refits on a rolling 365-day window -- a much harder task.
PatchTST (197k params) overfits faster than TFT (1.27M params) on short
training windows. The patch inductive bias does not compensate.

\subsection{Feature analysis: why it lost}

Post-mortem on the best config (patch24\_s24\_ctx1344). Four analyses;
artifacts in \texttt{reports/sensitivity/patchtst/}.

\textbf{Group ablation} (zero one input group, retrain, full 2-year
walk-forward, 3 seeds):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
ablated group & MAE (EUR/MWh) & $\Delta$MAE vs full & coverage 80\% \\
\midrule
--- (full model) & 23.61 $\pm$ 0.53 & 0 & 68.5\% \\
encoder (56 d price history) & 23.23 $\pm$ 0.10 & $-0.38$ & 73.2\% \\
calendar & 23.68 $\pm$ 0.43 & $+0.08$ & 71.4\% \\
TSO load forecast & 24.08 $\pm$ 0.55 & $+0.47$ & 70.7\% \\
anchor (lag-168 price) & 24.47 $\pm$ 0.30 & $+0.86$ & 67.2\% \\
solar forecast only & 26.67 $\pm$ 0.22 & $+3.07$ & 69.0\% \\
onshore wind forecast only & 27.72 $\pm$ 0.21 & $+4.11$ & 73.3\% \\
RES forecast (all) & 29.84 $\pm$ 0.36 & $+6.23$ & 74.0\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Two facts jump out.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The encoder is dead weight.} Removing the entire 56-day
        price history does not hurt ($-0.38$, within seed noise) and
        \emph{improves} coverage by 4.6~pp. PatchTST's core mechanism ---
        cheap attention over long history --- contributes nothing here.
        The model is an expensive MLP over tomorrow's covariates.
  \item \textbf{The RES forecast carries the skill} ($+6.2$ EUR/MWh).
        Without it the model is worse than naive (rMAE 1.07).
        Merit-order: renewables set the day-ahead price.
  \item \textbf{Within RES, wind beats solar} ($+4.1$ vs $+3.1$).
        Wind is the less predictable series, so its forecast carries
        more unique information; solar is partly recoverable from
        calendar and season. Individual deltas sum to 7.2 $>$ joint
        6.2 --- the two forecasts overlap (both proxy residual load).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Permutation vs ablation --- a teaching moment.} Permutation
importance (fixed model, shuffle one input) ranks the encoder FIRST
($\Delta$pinball $+0.18$, $\Delta$MAE $+26$ EUR/MWh). Ablation with
retraining ranks it last. Both are correct: permutation measures what
the trained model \emph{relies on}; ablation measures whether the
information is \emph{unique}. Price history is fully redundant with the
covariates --- the model uses it, but does not need it. Quoting only
permutation importance would have led to the wrong conclusion.

\textbf{Attention maps} (patch $=$ stride $=$ 24 h, so one patch $=$ one
day): top attended day ages are 0 (yesterday), 7, 12, 1 --- plausible
recency and weekly structure. But the map is nearly flat: weights span
0.014--0.022 against a uniform 1/56 $=$ 0.018. Plausible-looking
attention is not evidence of value; the ablation settles it.

\textbf{PCA}: raw 24-h price patches are low-rank (PC1--4 $=$ 93\% of
variance); the learned pooled representation is effectively
2-dimensional (PC1--2 $=$ 97\%). The encoder compresses 56 days into
almost nothing --- consistent with redundancy.

\subsection{Interview lines}

PatchTST (Nie et al. 2023) reduces attention cost from $O(T^2)$ to
$O((T/P)^2)$ by treating price subsequences as tokens. We swept 27
configs over patch length, stride, and context on the Polish day-ahead
price task.

Screening finding: 24-hour patches with 56-day context dominated.
One patch = one calendar day made weekday/holiday/seasonal cycles the
natural token-level signal.

Walk-forward finding: \textit{negative}. Best config (patch24, ctx1344)
MAE 22.98 EUR/MWh, rMAE 0.823 -- 16\% worse than TFT (19.71, rMAE 0.706)
and 29\% worse than champion LGBM (17.87, rMAE 0.640). Coverage 69.5\%
vs target 80\%. The patch inductive bias does not compensate for the
smaller model capacity on short training windows.

Feature-analysis line: group ablation with retraining showed the
56-day price-history encoder adds \emph{zero} skill --- all signal
sits in the known-future RES forecast ($+6.2$ EUR/MWh when removed).
Permutation importance on the fixed model said the opposite (encoder
ranked first) --- the classic reliance-vs-redundancy gap between the
two methods. The negative result is explained, not just observed.

Production lesson: a zero-variance column (Baltic offshore wind,
commissioned July 2026) drove val pinball from 0.14 to 879.
Fix: detect $\sigma < 10^{-4}$ in training, zero that column in all
sets instead of dividing by $10^{-6}$. TFT survived because LSTM and
GRN sigmoid gates saturate at extreme inputs; PatchTST's linear attention
amplified the $1.9 \times 10^7$ z-score directly.
