\section{Price attention campaign --- HPO + three-method interpretation}

The goal: test whether TFT's long-context attention closes the gap with
LEAR/LGBM that the screening tier (section 7) could not.
Method: 60-trial Optuna HPO over architecture AND context length, then
3-seed walk-forward to confirm the winner.

\subsection{HPO: what the search found (screening tier)}

60 trials over: \texttt{encoder\_hours} \{336, 672, 1008, 1344, 2016\},
\texttt{d\_model} \{32, 48, 64, 96, 128\}, \texttt{n\_heads} \{2, 4, 8\},
\texttt{lstm\_layers} \{1, 2\}, plus dropout, lr, batch.

Objective: validation pinball on a single temporal split
(train < 2026-01-01, val 2026-01-01 to present).

\textbf{Emerging pattern from top trials:}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{encoder\_hours = 1008} and \texttt{2016} are essentially
        tied (val $\approx 0.1184$). Longer context pays up to 42 days;
        beyond that, returns are flat on the screening split.
  \item \texttt{n\_heads = 8} dominates but \texttt{n\_heads = 2} can
        match it with the right regularization. Head count is less
        decisive than context length and dropout.
  \item \texttt{d\_model = 32} can match \texttt{d\_model = 64} ---
        the price signal's effective dimensionality is low.
\end{itemize}

Rule enforced throughout: HPO val is for \emph{selection only}.
Walk-forward numbers go into the results table.
Single-split HPO flatters nets by 0.6--0.9 pp (measured on the load task).

\subsection{Three-method interpretation comparison}

Three signals, three questions:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
Method & Model & Question & Cost \\
\hline
SHAP & LGBM & how much does each feature shift the output? & 1 min/day \\
Group ablation & LGBM & how much skill does each group add? & 1 backtest/group \\
VSN & TFT & which future covariates does the model attend to? & free \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{Rankings (top-5):}

SHAP: solar 18.7, price\_lag\_1d 14.1, wind\_on 8.4, tso\_load 7.9, price\_lag\_7d 6.1.

Ablation: res group $+3.5$ MAE, price\_lags $+2.8$, calendar $+0.3$, tso $+0.3$.

VSN (future covariates only): solar 0.217, tso\_load 0.170, wind\_on 0.126,
doy\_sin 0.098, anchor\_lag168 0.097.

\textbf{Key insight.} All three methods agree: solar forecast is the #1 price
driver. The agreement is non-trivial --- the methods measure different things.
SHAP is local gradient. Ablation is marginal skill contribution.
VSN is attention weight over future covariates.

\textbf{The one disagreement.} Price lags are SHAP \#2 but VSN does not
rank them highly, because VSN only sees \emph{future} covariates
(calendar, RES, TSO, anchor). The encoder LSTM absorbs autoregressive
price information directly --- the architecture is designed so that
price lags enter through the recurrent path, not the selection network.
The VSN ranking is conditional: ``among known-future features, which matter?''
SHAP and ablation answer unconditionally. Understanding the scope of each
method prevents misreading.

\subsection{Interview line}

``We ran 60-trial HPO over architecture AND context length. The winner
used 42 days of price history and small attention (n\_heads=2, d32).

on the top feature (solar forecast) but differ on how to count price lags.
Understanding \emph{why} they differ is more useful than picking one.''
