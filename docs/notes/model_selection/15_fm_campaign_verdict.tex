\section{Foundation-model campaign verdict --- Chronos, TimesFM, Moirai}

\subsection{Setup}

Three zero-shot foundation models through the identical 2-year
backtest. No training on our data. CQR on top where bands needed
calibration. Fairness footnote as always: zero-shot models are
covariate-blind (except \texttt{moirai\_cov}); the champion sees
RES, TSO load and calendar.

\subsection{Verdicts}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Chronos-Bolt wins the FM race} (rMAE 0.787). It is the
      pre-declared ``best FM'' ensemble member.
\item \textbf{TimesFM's raw bands are remarkably calibrated}: 80.7\%
      coverage without any conformal step. Chronos and Moirai need
      CQR. If you need honest bands with zero infrastructure,
      TimesFM is the pick.
\item \textbf{Moirai's covariate mode is a clean negative}: 1.2 MAE
      worse than its own univariate variant, DM-significant.
      Covariate skill needs training (learning note 24).
\item \textbf{All FMs beat naive by a wide margin, none touches the
      champion.} The 4--7 MAE gap is what covariates plus local
      training buy.
\item Fine-tune gate (rMAE $< 0.75$) stays closed. No FM cleared it.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What survives into the product}

Chronos joins the CRPS ensemble as the diversity member
(model-selection note 16). TimesFM and Moirai are benchmark rows
and interview material, not product components.

\subsection{Interview line}

``I benchmarked three foundation-model families on identical
walk-forwards. Chronos won, TimesFM had the best-calibrated raw
bands, and Moirai's covariate mode was significantly worse than
its own univariate mode. One sentence: zero-shot is a strong free
baseline, a diversity donor for ensembles, and no champion.''
