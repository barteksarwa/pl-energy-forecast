\section{Price Task: LEAR vs LightGBM}

\subsection{Starting point}

The price task began with one hard constraint: any model must beat LEAR.
LEAR (LASSO-Estimated AutoRegression, Ziel \& Weron 2018) is the
reproducible benchmark in the EPF (electricity price forecasting) literature.
Every published paper that claims to beat a baseline must beat LEAR.
Our rMAE target: below 0.660.

We implemented LEAR from the specification in Uniejewski, Weron \& Ziel (2018):
per-hour models, robust standardisation ($\asinh((p-\text{med})/\text{MAD})$),
price lags at $24h$, $48h$, $168h$, same-day-last-week mean, load forecast.
Three variants were tested; only the third matched the literature result.
The failed variants are documented in the LEAR model card.

\subsection{LEAR result after fuel features}

Base LEAR (no fuel signal): MAE 18.5, winter bias $-15.9$ EUR/MWh in January.
Fuel features (TTF gas proxy, EUA CO2 proxy): MAE 18.24, winter bias $-4.6$ EUR/MWh.

The improvement is concentrated in high-gas months: November--February.
In the summer months, the fuel features add noise (gas not marginal).
LASSO handles this: it zeros the fuel coefficients in summer-hour models
where gas price variation does not co-move with price.

Mechanism confirmed: fuel features improve exactly where the merit-order
predicts (when gas is the marginal unit). This is the right kind of improvement.

\subsection{LightGBM challenger}

LightGBM received the same feature set as LEAR plus interaction terms
(solar $\times$ demand, wind $\times$ season, price lag $\times$ gas regime).
LGBM can learn these nonlinear interactions; LASSO cannot.

Results on the same 2-year walk-forward (2024-07-16 to present):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
Model & MAE & rMAE & Coverage 80\% & Spike MAE \\
\midrule
Naive (same hour -1d) & 27.94 & 1.000 & 53.1\% & 77.6 \\
LEAR + fuel + conformal & 18.24 & 0.653 & 79.5\% & 71.0 \\
LGBM + conformal & 17.87 & 0.640 & 78.7\% & 60.6 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

LGBM wins on MAE by 0.37 EUR/MWh (2.0\%).
LEAR wins on coverage (79.5 vs 78.7\%).
LGBM wins on spike MAE by 10.4 EUR/MWh (15\%).

The spike result is the clearest LGBM advantage. Trees can learn
nonlinear combinations (low wind AND high demand AND summer)
that LASSO cannot represent.

\subsection{Why LEAR stays as incumbent}

Two reasons:

\textbf{1. Backtest is not production.}
LGBM's 0.37 EUR/MWh MAE advantage is real but modest.
On a 14-day shadow window it may not replicate.
The desk rule: promotion requires evidence from the shadow window,
not just the backtest. LGBM's shadow gate starts after TFT walks forward.

\textbf{2. Incumbency cost.}
Changing the published model resets the daily-report track record.
We are on day 1 of the shadow tally. Swapping now would reset to day 0.
The tally is the product; resetting it is not free.

Decision logged in DECISIONS.md (2026-07-16).

\subsection{SHAP interpretation}

LGBM gives us SHAP; LEAR gives us LASSO coefficients.
Both agree on the top driver: solar forecast.

\toprule

\midrule
1 & solar\_fcst\_mw 18.7 & solar\_fcst\_mw (high in summer) \\
2 & price\_lag\_1d 14.1 & price\_lag\_168h (all hours) \\
3 & wind\_on\_fcst\_mw 8.4 & tso\_forecast\_mw \\

Solar ranks \#1 in both interpretability frameworks on a task where
we did not engineer that signal. It emerges from the merit order: PV
at midday dispatches before coal, suppressing the price.
The model learned the physics.

\subsection{Known limitations}

Both LEAR and LGBM share the same structural weakness:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Spike MAE 60--70 EUR/MWh}: 3$\times$ the pooled MAE.
        Spikes require unit-level outage data. The ENTSO-E UMM aggregate
        feature was evaluated and rejected (flat, \S10 in DECISIONS.md).
  \item \textbf{Negative prices}: 798 hours in the test set had price $< 0$.
        LGBM's quantile regression undercovers the lower tail.
        Conformal calibration partially fixes this; CQR would do it properly.
  \item \textbf{Gas regime changes}: TTF proxy is a 6-month lagged ENTSO-E
        series, not real-time gas price. A live TTF feed would improve the
        speed of regime-shift detection.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What the TFT verdict will settle}

The open question is whether attention over 8+ weeks of price history
adds information beyond the 168h lag that both LEAR and LGBM already use.
Screening (note 07) showed long context IS real: rMAE improves monotonically
with encoder length up to 1344h. But the screening val number (0.1176)
is 0.6--0.9pp optimistic vs walk-forward.

After HPO + 3-seed walk-forward, we will know:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Does TFT beat LGBM on MAE?
  \item Does TFT beat LGBM on spike MAE (attention as implicit regime memory)?
  \item Does TFT's coverage degrade without conformal (raw quantile training)?
\end{itemize}

If TFT wins: shadow gate, 14-day tally, promotion decision.
If TFT loses: this note records the honest verdict.
Either outcome advances the owner's knowledge.
\emph{An honest loss is worth more than a hidden one.}
