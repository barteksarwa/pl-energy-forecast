\section{GPD tail on the conformal band --- tested, rejected}

\subsection{The idea}

Price spikes live in the upper tail. Our band is calibrated with
symmetric CQR. The empirical tail quantile uses 90 days of scores.
Few extreme points support it. Extreme value theory offers a fix:
fit a generalized Pareto distribution (GPD) to the exceedances.
Read the tail quantile from the fitted curve, not one order statistic.
This is the peaks-over-threshold (POT) method.

\subsection{The setup}

Hybrid band. Lower tail keeps the symmetric CQR offset. Upper tail:
threshold at the 80th percentile of $y - P90$ scores. GPD fitted on
exceedances. Guards: at least 30 exceedances, shape $\xi < 1$,
fallback to the empirical quantile.

Gate, declared before the run: spike coverage must beat both CQR
variants by 3 points. Pooled coverage must stay in 78--82\%.
Winkler score within +2\%.

\subsection{The result}

\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\hline
Method & LGBM spike cover & LEAR spike cover & Verdict \\
\hline
Symmetric CQR & 51.3\% & 55.6\% & shipped \\
Asymmetric CQR & 51.3\% & 56.3\% & rejected \\
GPD upper tail & 51.3\% & 56.2\% & rejected \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

\vspace{0.5em}
GPD moved spike coverage by half a point at best. Gate failed.

\subsection{Why it failed}

The empirical quantile was not the problem. With 2{,}160 hourly
scores per window, the 90th percentile is stable. The real problem:
spike errors are \emph{conditional}. The model does not see the
spike coming. Its P50 sits low, its P90 sits low, and no constant
band width fixes a miss that is specific to one afternoon.

Worked example. A calm week gives scores near $-5$ (inside the band).
One scarcity evening gives $+120$. A wider band that covers the $+120$
hour also wastes width on 160 calm hours. Coverage barely moves.
The information must enter \emph{before} the band: a spike
probability per hour, from features (tight reserve margin, low wind,
high load). That is the spike classifier.

\subsection{Interview lines}

``I tested an EVT tail on the conformal band. It failed a
pre-declared gate. The lesson: our spike errors are conditional,
not a calibration problem --- so I built a spike classifier instead
of a wider band.''

``Three tail methods, one survivor: plain symmetric CQR. Simple
methods win when the failure mode is elsewhere.''
