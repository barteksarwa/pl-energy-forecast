\section{TFT with long context --- tested, helps, loses anyway}

Owner hypothesis (2026-07-17): attention models can digest MONTHS of
history, while our tabular models see at most 7-day lags --- maybe the
missing spike skill lives in that longer context. Fair hypothesis,
directly testable. We ran it the same night.

\subsection{Setup}

TFT (Lim et al.\ 2021: variable selection + LSTM + temporal attention,
quantile heads), price target, encoder lengths 1 / 4 / 12 weeks,
d\_model 64, one seed (screening tier), walk-forward over the last 180
days with monthly refits. Known-future covariates: calendar, wind+solar
forecast, TSO load forecast. Same information set as the tabular
models --- only the PAST WINDOW differs.

\subsection{Result (identical 4320-hour window)}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
model & MAE & rMAE & spike MAE \\
\hline
LEAR + conformal & \textbf{18.5} & \textbf{0.66} & 52.2 \\
LightGBM + conformal & 18.7 & 0.67 & \textbf{49.8} \\
TFT, 12-week context & 24.1 & 0.86 & 57.8 \\
TFT, 4-week context & 24.5 & 0.88 & 54.3 \\
TFT, 1-week context & 25.9 & 0.93 & 72.4 \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Two findings, and they point in opposite directions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The owner's mechanism is real.} rMAE improves
        monotonically with context length. The attention model DOES
        extract something from months of history that a week misses.
  \item \textbf{And it is nowhere near enough.} The best TFT trails the
        tabular models by $\sim$30\% on the same window and does not
        fix spikes either. Matches Lago et al.\ (2021): properly built
        LEAR/GBM are brutal baselines for day-ahead prices.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The bug worth remembering}

First run produced rMAE 2.0--2.5 --- WORSE than copying yesterday. Cause:
prediction-time samples skipped covariate standardization, so a network
trained on z-scored inputs received a raw-scale TSO column ($\sim$20{,}000)
and saturated. One line of preprocessing asymmetry between fit and
predict turned a working model into noise. Deep pipelines fail SILENTLY
--- nothing crashed, the numbers were just wrong. Tabular pipelines with
shared feature matrices cannot have this class of bug, which is itself
an argument in production.

\subsection{Verdict}

Long context: real, small, insufficient. The binding constraint for
spikes remains missing information (outages, fuel, cross-border), not
architecture. The outage-feature test ran the same day. Screening
caveats: one seed, one size; a tuned ensemble would close some gap ---
the literature says not all of it.

Interview line: ``We tested the owner's long-context hypothesis
directly: attention over 12 weeks beats attention over 1 week --- the
mechanism exists --- but a linear model with the right features still
beats both by 30\%. Context is not information.''
