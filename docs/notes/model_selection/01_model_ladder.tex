\section{The model ladder: cheap to expensive}

Rule: move one rung up only when the current rung is beaten on the headline
metric (skill over seasonal naive, and pinball loss for quantiles).
Verdicts get filled in as backtests land (M3--M5). Empty verdict = not tested yet.

\subsection{The ladder for load forecasting}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lllp{4.5cm}}
\toprule
Rung & Model & Cost to run & Use case \\
\midrule
1 & Seasonal naive        & none    & floor; sanity check; DST debugging \\
2 & Linear / ridge        & seconds & ``is nonlinearity needed at all?'' \\
3 & LASSO-AR (LEAR-style) & seconds & industry price baseline; feature selection built in \\
4 & LightGBM quantile     & minutes & production candidate; nonlinear, interpretable via SHAP \\
5 & LSTM                  & minutes--hours & sequence memory; known-future covariates \\
6 & Small transformer     & hours   & challenger only; needs lots of data \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{What each rung needs (data availability)}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Rungs 1--2: load history only. Work from day one.
  \item Rung 3: load + calendar. Stable with $\sim$1 year of history.
  \item Rung 4: load + calendar + weather. Wants 2--3 years to see all seasons.
  \item Rungs 5--6: same data, but hungrier. Below $\sim$2 years they usually
        overfit and lose to rung 4. Literature agrees; we will verify on PL data.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Known priors from literature (to verify, not trust)}

\begin{itemize}
  \item One-step-ahead metrics cannot separate models on smooth signals;
        everything looks like persistence. Judge at the full 24--38h horizon.
  \item LEAR-type linear models beat most deep nets on day-ahead \emph{price};
        gradient boosting is the strongest tabular default for \emph{load}.
  \item Deep nets win mainly when there is a lot of data and complex
        covariate interactions. PL hourly load is $\sim$26k points in 3 years ---
        modest.
\end{itemize}
