\subsection{The headline: we beat the TSO by combining with it}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}

\end{center}

The TSO forecast publishes at our 09:00 D-1 cutoff, so it is a legal
known-future input (DECISIONS 2026-07-15). Adding it turns our models into
\emph{forecast combiners} --- and the combination beats the incumbent by 8\%
MAE. Classic literature result (combination gains of 5--15\%): the desk
truth that you post-process the TSO, not fight it blind.

Why ridge wins: an optimal combination of a strong, nearly unbiased
forecast with residual predictors is almost linear. Trees and nets have
nothing nonlinear left to model, and pay variance for trying.

\subsection{Questions closed tonight}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Bigger nets? No.} Capacity curve complete: 3.21 / 3.00 /
        3.14 / 3.15 / 3.28\% for 29k / 106k / 409k / 1.6M / 6.4M params
        (screening). Peak at 106k; data-limited, not capacity-limited.
  \item \textbf{Origin augmentation? Hurts.} 4 origins per day: 3.66--3.73\%
        vs 3.00\% baseline. Shifted origins train a task never evaluated.
        Negative result, logged, not to be re-derived.
  \item \textbf{Ladder (screening).} vanilla 4.7\% $\approx$ BiLSTM 4.6\%
        (history-only rungs) $\ll$ enc\_dec 3.00\% $<$
        \textbf{Luong attention 2.88\%} (stable across h64--h256).
        Covariates dominate; attention adds a real but modest edge.
        Attention walk-forward $\pm$ TSO queued after the campaign.
  \item \textbf{Screening flatters nets.} enc\_dec: 3.00\% screening
        $\to$ 3.67\% walk-forward. Single-split scores rank ideas;
        only walk-forward numbers enter the README.
  \item \textbf{LightGBM tuning: marginal.} Both informed configs 2.14\%
        vs 2.16\% default (with TSO). The signal is in the features,
        not the hyperparameters.
\end{itemize}
