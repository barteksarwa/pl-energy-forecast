\section{Intraday --- the correction market}

The day-ahead auction fixes tomorrow's schedule at noon today. Then
reality drifts: the wind forecast updates, a unit trips, it gets colder.
Intraday is where positions get corrected before the balancing market
punishes what is left.

\subsection{Mechanics}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Continuous trading}, not an auction: orders match the
        moment a buy and a sell cross, like an order book on an exchange.
        Prices move all day.
  \item Poland trades on TGE (RDB) and is coupled to Europe through
        \textbf{SIDC/XBID}, the single intraday coupling --- a shared
        European order book, capacity permitting.
  \item Opens in the afternoon of D-1 after day-ahead results; trading
        runs until shortly before delivery (cross-border gate closure is
        60 minutes before the delivery period; local trading closer).
  \item Products down to 15 minutes --- matching the 15-minute imbalance
        settlement, so a quarter-hour ramp can be hedged precisely.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The timeline, end to end}

For delivery hour H on day D:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item D-1, 09:00 --- our load-forecast cutoff; PSE publishes its own.
  \item D-1, 12:00 CET --- day-ahead auction (SDAC) gate closure.
        Price fixed for every hour of D. This is what our price models
        forecast.
  \item D-1 afternoon $\to$ D, H$-$1h --- intraday trading corrects
        positions as forecasts update.
  \item H --- delivery. Whatever is still wrong settles at the
        15-minute imbalance price.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why a desk cares}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The day-ahead\,$\to$\,intraday price spread is a tradeable view on
        \emph{forecast updates}: if you know wind will disappoint before
        the market does, you buy intraday early.
  \item Intraday liquidity is thinner than day-ahead; execution matters
        as much as the forecast.
  \item For us: a natural Phase-3+ extension is re-forecasting with
        fresher weather during day D --- the intraday desk's core loop.
        Same features, shorter horizon, faster cadence.
\end{itemize}

Interview line: ``Day-ahead is a forecast frozen at noon. Intraday is
the market's live correction of that forecast. The money is in knowing
which corrections are coming before they print.''
