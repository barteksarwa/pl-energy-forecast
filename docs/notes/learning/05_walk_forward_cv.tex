\section{Walk-forward validation}

\subsection{Why normal cross-validation is forbidden here}

Standard $k$-fold CV shuffles data randomly. On time series that trains on
Friday to predict the previous Monday. Two failures:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Leakage.} Load is autocorrelated; a training hour 3 hours
        after a test hour carries most of its information. Scores inflate.
  \item \textbf{Wrong question.} Production always predicts \emph{forward}.
        Only a forward-in-time evaluation estimates production error.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{How walk-forward works}

Mimic the desk, day by day:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Pick a test day $D$.
  \item Train on a trailing window that ends before $D$
        (ours: 365 days, so the model always sees every season once).
  \item Forecast all hours of $D$ under the 09:00 D-1 cutoff.
  \item Advance one day. Repeat over the whole test period.
\end{enumerate}

Every test day is scored by a model that knew nothing about it or anything
after it. Our engine: \texttt{src/evaluation/backtest.py}.

\subsection{Two engineering choices worth defending}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Refit every 7 days, not daily.} Refitting LASSO daily over a
        year of test days is slow and changes little. Between refits, the last
        model keeps forecasting --- exactly how desks run models.
  \item \textbf{Rolling window, not expanding.} A fixed 365-day window forgets
        old regimes (fuel-cost shifts, post-2022 demand changes). An expanding
        window remembers everything, including what no longer applies.
        Debatable both ways; we test the choice in M3 rather than argue it.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The menu of validation schemes}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lp{6.5cm}l}
\toprule
Scheme & What it does & Verdict here \\
\midrule
Random $k$-fold & shuffles hours across folds & banned (leakage) \\
Single holdout & one fixed test block at the end & too weak: one period, one regime \\
Blocked CV + embargo & contiguous folds, gap around test block & fine for tuning; finance standard \\
Expanding walk-forward & train = everything before test day & remembers old regimes forever \\
Rolling walk-forward & train = last $N$ days before test day & \textbf{ours} ($N=365$) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Why rolling: it mirrors desk operations (models retire old data), adapts to
regime change (post-2022 demand shifts), and one year of window still sees
every season. Rolling vs expanding is an empirical question, not doctrine ---
the M3 campaign runs the ablation and the table decides. Blocked CV
returns when we tune LightGBM hyperparameters \emph{inside} training windows.

\subsection{Is shuffling whole blocks allowed?}

Shuffle days or weeks as units, keep order inside each block: that is
blocked CV. Whether it is valid depends on one question ---
\textbf{are the blocks approximately independent?}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Independent-event data: yes.} Isolated transients or triggered
        events with no long-range correlation (e.g.\ fuel-cell transients from
        separate experiments) are exchangeable units. Shuffling blocks is
        legitimate there and gives better data efficiency than one holdout.
  \item \textbf{National load: mostly no, for three reasons.}
        (1) Blocks are strongly dependent --- week $t$ correlates with week
        $t{+}1$ far beyond the boundary hours (an embargo only fixes edges).
        (2) Regime leakage: training on 2026 blocks to predict 2025 teaches
        the model demand levels and habits that did not exist yet.
        (3) Our features cross block borders by construction --- lag-672
        reaches four weeks back, straight into other blocks.
  \item \textbf{The deeper point:} block shuffling answers ``can the model
        interpolate between periods it has seen?'' Production asks ``can it
        extrapolate forward?'' Only forward-in-time evaluation answers the
        production question. Shuffled-block scores are systematically
        optimistic on trending, seasonal series.
\end{itemize}

Where we DO shuffle: training \emph{samples} (whole forecast days) within a
fixed training window are shuffled every epoch when fitting neural nets.
That only reorders gradient updates inside data the model may legally see;
the train/test frontier stays temporal.

\subsection{Proof, not promise}

Test in \texttt{tests/test\_backtest.py}: corrupt the target with garbage
($10^9$) everywhere after the test start; predictions must not change.
If training ever touched the future, they would.

\subsection{Interview line}

``My backtest walks forward like the desk lives: trailing training window,
weekly refits, the 09:00 cutoff enforced every single day. And I prove no
future data leaks by corrupting it and showing nothing changes.''
