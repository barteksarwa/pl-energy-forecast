\section{The balancing market (rynek bilansuj\k{a}cy)}

The market where forecast errors become invoices. Everything in this
repo ultimately optimizes against this mechanism.

\subsection{The mechanics}

You told the market (via the day-ahead auction) you would consume 100~MWh
between 14:00 and 15:00. You actually consumed 110. Someone had to
generate the missing 10~MWh in real time. The TSO (PSE) bought it for you
--- and bills you at the \emph{imbalance price}, which in stressed hours is
far worse than what day-ahead would have cost. That difference, summed
over every settlement period, is the cost of bad forecasting.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Every market participant belongs to a balancing group under a
        \emph{Balance Responsible Party} (BRP) that settles its imbalances.
  \item The TSO keeps the system balanced by activating bids from
        \emph{Balancing Service Providers} (BSPs) --- flexible plants,
        storage, demand response.
  \item Imbalance = your declared schedule minus your metered reality,
        per settlement period.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The 2024 Polish reform --- know this for interviews}

PSE reformed the balancing market on 2024-06-14 (the same date our PSE
API data starts --- not a coincidence, the API came with the reform):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{15-minute settlement} replaced hourly. Forecast errors
        now hurt at quarter-hour resolution; a flat hourly profile no
        longer hides an intra-hour ramp.
  \item \textbf{Scarcity pricing}: when reserves run thin, an adder
        pushes the imbalance price above the most expensive activated
        bid. Prices can reach thousands of PLN/MWh in tight hours.
  \item Alignment with the EU Electricity Balancing Guideline: BSP/BRP
        role separation, standard products, path to EU platforms
        (PICASSO/MARI) for automatic reserves.
\end{itemize}

Our \texttt{price\_balancing.parquet} holds the post-reform settlement
price (\texttt{crb-rozl}). Comparing it with the day-ahead price shows
the premium reality charges for being wrong.

\subsection{Why a desk cares}

\begin{itemize}
  \item A retailer's load forecast error settles at the imbalance price.
        1\% MAPE on a large Polish portfolio is millions of PLN per year.
  \item A trader can deliberately carry a position INTO balancing if
        they forecast the imbalance price better than the market --- risky,
        regulated, but it is a real strategy. It needs exactly the models
        this repo builds.
  \item The day-ahead vs balancing spread is the price of certainty; its
        statistics justify every euro spent on better forecasts.
\end{itemize}

Interview line: ``Forecast quality is not a KPI, it is a cash flow. The
balancing market is where the cash leaves.''
