\section{Forecast value in EUR --- battery arbitrage and capture rate}

\subsection{Why translate MAE into money}

MAE says how wrong a forecast is. It does not say what the error
costs. A trading desk asks the second question. The cleanest bridge
is a storage asset: a battery that buys cheap hours and sells
expensive ones. Its profit depends directly on forecast quality.

\subsection{The setup}

A small battery: 1 MW power, 2 MWh capacity, 85\% round-trip
efficiency, one cycle per day. Day-ahead market only.

The daily routine mirrors the forecast product:
\begin{enumerate}
\item At D$-$1, take the model's P50 price forecast for day D.
\item Solve for the best charge/discharge schedule (a small linear
      program: maximize revenue subject to power, capacity and
      cycle limits).
\item Day D arrives. Settle the committed schedule at ACTUAL prices.
\end{enumerate}
The schedule is decided on the forecast. The money is made or lost
on the actuals. Exactly the leakage discipline of the backtests.

\subsection{Capture rate}

Run the same optimizer on the actual prices: that is perfect
foresight, the ceiling nobody beats.
\[
\text{capture rate} = \frac{\text{P\&L from forecast schedule}}
                           {\text{P\&L with perfect foresight}}
\]
1.0 means the forecast lost nothing. Our 2-year numbers:

\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\hline
Schedule source & EUR/day & Capture & MAE \\
\hline
Perfect foresight & 221 & 1.000 & 0 \\
Ensemble (CRPS+CQR) & 205 & 0.924 & 17.34 \\
Champion LGBM & 202 & 0.914 & 17.87 \\
LEAR & 201 & 0.908 & 18.24 \\
Chronos zero-shot & 197 & 0.890 & 21.99 \\
TimesFM zero-shot & 195 & 0.880 & 22.52 \\
Naive (yesterday) & 180 & 0.813 & 27.96 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

\subsection{The surprising compression}

Look at the naive row. A forecast that is 10.6 EUR/MWh worse in MAE
still captures 81\% of the value. Why: the battery only needs the
\emph{ordering} of hours --- when is it cheap, when is it expensive.
Yesterday's prices already rank today's hours quite well.
Level errors cancel; a schedule survives them.

So 10.6 MAE of skill buys 11 points of capture. The marginal value
of accuracy is real but compressed. This is a general lesson:
\textbf{the value of a forecast depends on the decision it feeds,
not on the error metric.} A storage trader needs shape. A retailer
hedging volume needs level. Same forecast, different worth.

\subsection{Honest scope}

Day-ahead only. A real desk re-trades intraday and captures more.
No grid fees, no degradation cost, no market impact. The number is
a clean comparator between models, not a business case.

\subsection{Interview line}

``I valued my forecasts with a battery-arbitrage backtest: schedule
on the P50 at D$-$1, settle at actuals. My ensemble captures 92\% of
perfect-foresight value. The instructive part: naive captures 81\% ---
storage only needs hour ordering, so accuracy value is compressed.
Metric improvements must be priced in the decision, not the MAE.''
